Disclaimer

What this atlas is and is not.

The Green Grid Atlas is an open-data story about the Dutch electricity grid, built from energy-charts.info (Fraunhofer ISE, CC BY 4.0; day-ahead prices from Bundesnetzagentur | SMARD.de, CC BY 4.0) and Open-Meteo weather (CC BY 4.0). It refreshes daily from a committed snapshot; it is snapshot-fresh, never live.

Computed carbon intensity uses the generation mix and does not attribute imports. The risk indicators are derived indicators, not a grid-security assessment. Nothing here is investment, switching, or grid-operations advice.

Method and sources

Every figure on this page traces to the committed snapshot, which records its source, license and retrieval date for each series.

Sourcing rule

Every figure on this page is fetched by scripts/backfill.mjs (once, full history) and scripts/snapshot.mjs (daily, 12:35 UTC) from the cited open data sources, politely paced (one request at a time, ≥ 31 s per endpoint, Retry-After honored), and stored with provenance in public/data/snapshot.json. Nothing statistical is hand-entered; all derived values are computed at render time or snapshot time from snapshot fields by the shared rule modules in src/grid. No API call ever happens from the client.

Basis disclosure (verbatim)

Computed carbon intensity uses the generation mix: domestic production types times the emission-factor table, divided by load. Imports are not attributed — cross-border flows carry no fuel mix, so import-heavy hours show a lower computed intensity than the power actually consumed would cause. This limitation applies everywhere intensity is shown (the intensity layer label, the green window card captions, the coda).

The green window rule (verbatim)

The green window is computed from the trailing 30 days of hourly values (price and renewable share of generation), each hour independent. A green hour has price at or below the 30th percentile of the trailing price distribution AND renewable share at or above the 70th percentile of the trailing renewable-share distribution. A red hour is the exact inverse through the same code path: price at or above the 70th percentile AND renewable share at or below the 30th percentile. For each day, the contiguous hour bands qualifying are collected; the rendered window is the modal recurring band (the band shape most frequent in the trailing window), displayed with its frequency share (share of trailing days having that band). Single qualifying hours outside bands are shown on the heatmap but do not form the window. Percentiles use linear interpolation on the sorted hourly sample. Basis: renewable share of generation (NL generation mix); price is the NL day-ahead price.

Constants: trailing 30 days, green price ≤ p30 / renewables ≥ p70, red price ≥ p70 / renewables ≤ p30. They live only in src/grid/green-window.ts and change only by editing that file; any change is stated in the PR description.

Risk indicator rules

  • Negative-price hours: Share of trailing-30-day hours with day-ahead price below 0 EUR/MWh. Watch at ≥ 1%, high at ≥ 5%.
  • Red-hour share: Share of trailing-30-day hours qualifying as red hours under the green window rule (price ≥ 70th percentile AND renewable share of generation ≤ 30th percentile, same code path as the green window). Watch at ≥ 20%, high at ≥ 35%.
  • Load covered by net imports: Over the trailing 30 days: Σ max(0, net physical import in GW × 1000) / Σ load in MW — the share of Dutch load covered by net imports (cross-border physical flows, imports positive). Watch at ≥ 25%, high at ≥ 40%.
  • Price spread (p90−p10): Trailing-30-day hourly price spread: 90th minus 10th percentile of day-ahead price, EUR/MWh. Watch at ≥ 150 EUR/MWh, high at ≥ 250 EUR/MWh.
  • Longest Dunkelflaute run: Longest consecutive run of hours in the trailing 90 days with wind onshore + wind offshore + solar below 20% of load. Watch at ≥ 24 h, high at ≥ 48 h.

Indicators derived from open data; not a grid-security assessment..

DST rule (verbatim)

All grids render in Europe/Amsterdam local time. The two 23-hour and 25-hour days each year render their actual hour count — 23 or 25 columns — with the missing spring-forward hour shown as an explicit gap cell and the doubled fall-back 02:00 shown as two labelled columns (02:00 +02:00 and 02:00 +01:00). Nothing is ever silently shifted.

Units

Prices EUR/MWh (day-ahead, NL bidding zone). Production, load, residual load MW. Cross-border physical flows GW, imports positive / exports negative. Renewable shares percent. Computed intensity g CO2-eq/kWh. Volumes TWh. Weather: wind speed 10 m km/h, shortwave radiation W/m², temperature 2 m °C. Timestamps are interval starts in Europe/Amsterdam with explicit UTC offset.

Suppression and gap rendering

Missing or suppressed source values are stored as null and rendered as grey gap cells with a "no data" label — never as zero, never interpolated. The heatmap colour scale spans the 5th–95th percentile of the displayed values; the legend prints both ends.

AI disclosure

The “AI insight (Experimental)” captions are generated at snapshot time by Mistral (mistral-small-latest, temperature 0.2) from the same aggregates that power the visual, gated by SHA-256 context signatures, validated so every numeral comes from the context (numerals-only guidance, prose-ratio lint), capped at two calls per visual and sixteen per run, and hidden when generation fails. They render verbatim as static text; no AI call happens in the browser. Key lives only in the MISTRAL_API_KEY Actions secret.
